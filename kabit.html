ਕਬਿੱਤ (ka-bit, translation: poem)

This is a personal page, where I explore the beauty of poetry - ਕਬਿਤ. Please avoid this page if you are not interested in poetry, or if you are here by mistake (this page isn't supposed to be available).

ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ ਵਰਗਿਆਂ ਲੋਕਾਂ ਨੂੰ ਖੁਦ ਵਿਚ ਜੋੜ ਲੈਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ 
ਮਿਲੇ ਗਹਿਰਾ ਕਿਤੇ ਲਿਖਿਆ ਤਾਂ ਵਰਕਾ ਮੋੜ ਲੈਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ
Translation: I connect with people like books. If I find something deep, I fold the page to remember it.
ਕਬਿੱਤ - Kabit - is a specific poetic form, often associated with the writings of Bhai Gurdas.
I am in no way claiming to be a poet, but I do enjoy writing poetry in my free time. I have been writing poetry since I was a child, and it has always been a way for me to express my thoughts and feelings. I find solace in the rhythm and flow of words even when they miss their mark, and I hope that you will too.

This is collection of poems, songs, and quotes that interest me. Some written by me, most are written by others that in some way have moved me. Some of these are in Punjabi, and I can try to provide a translation in English wherever possible.

  • TriggerWarning: politics (April 18, 2020)

    If I remember correctly when we were quite young, like kids, there were emotions and talks of even friending the rival country Pakistan at some levels or another, having a sense of fraternity around the Indian Peninsula. But now you see, even inside the country Hindus and Muslims are not able to see one another with respect. I am not saying that any particular faction is wrong or right, but this isn't the India I grew up in, there is a drastic change. A change I didn't even want to acknowledge, A change that is not the reflection of the World's largest democracy, A change that has a potential to bring the sovereign state of India to its knees. Again Let me make myself clear that this is not a politically triggering or religiously biased statement. And I may be way wrong here. But isn't this exactly what happened in pre-colonized India, like there was a great lack of unity, a great lack of tolerance for same side of people who were getting (then) attacked by the Britishers.

    I may be too young or too inexperienced to write this stuff and maybe half or even whole of it is wrong. Yet this is how I feel and I just wanted to say this to someone.


    P.S. (July 10, 2025) I love how my view about unity hasn't changed, but hate that cynicism has grown to a point where it feels like a lost cause. I don't know if I can ever get back to that naive mindset. I'd love to. But now I know that unity is not just a feeling, it's a practice that requires effort and commitment from everyone involved, which is something I am willing to work on, but the world seems a much harsher place for these ideals.
